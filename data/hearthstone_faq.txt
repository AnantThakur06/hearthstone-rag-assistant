HEARTHSTONE COFFEE CO.
FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
Last updated: 2 July 2026

There are three of us. We roast in Portland, Oregon, we pack every order by hand, and we
answer every email ourselves. This document is everything people ask us most, written out
properly so you do not have to wait on a reply. If your question is not here, write to us at
hello@hearthstonecoffee.com and we will answer, usually within one working day.

SECTION 1: ABOUT US
================================================================================

Q: Who are you?
A: Hearthstone Coffee Co. is a three person roastery in Portland, Oregon. Maya roasts,
Dev handles green buying and quality control, and Rosa runs everything else including the
inbox you are probably trying to avoid emailing. We started in 2019 out of a garage and
moved into our current workshop in 2022.

Q: Where is your roastery?
A: 1140 SE Ankeny Street, Portland, Oregon. The workshop is not open to the public and we
do not have a cafe, so please do not turn up expecting a flat white. We do open the doors
once a month for a public cupping.

Q: What is a cupping and can I come?
A: A cupping is a structured tasting where you smell and slurp several coffees side by side
from identical bowls. It is how the trade evaluates coffee and it is genuinely fun. We run
one on the first Saturday of every month at 10am. It is free, it lasts about 90 minutes, and
there are 12 places. Dates and signup go out in our newsletter first, and leftover places go
on the site.

Q: Do you have a shop or cafe?
A: No. We are online only, plus a handful of wholesale cafe accounts around Portland. The
full list of cafes serving our coffee is on our Stockists page.

Q: Are you hiring?
A: Occasionally. When we are, it is posted on the site and in the newsletter. We do not keep
a general applications file, sorry.

SECTION 2: OUR COFFEE
================================================================================

Q: Where do you roast your beans?
A: All of it, by hand, in our Portland workshop on a 20kg Loring. We roast in small batches
on Tuesdays and Fridays.

Q: How fresh is the coffee when it ships?
A: We roast to order. Your coffee is roasted on the roast day following your order and ships
the same day it comes off the roaster, so it is almost always less than 24 hours old when it
leaves us. The exact roast date is stamped on the back of every bag.

Q: When is coffee at its best?
A: Between 5 and 30 days after the roast date. Before day 5 the coffee is still degassing
carbon dioxide and tastes uneven and gassy. After about six weeks it is not bad for you, it
just goes flat and loses aroma. There is no safety date on coffee, only a flavour one.

Q: What coffees do you sell?
A: Four blends and seven single origins.

The blends:
- Hearth Blend. Our house coffee. Medium roast, chocolate and orange, forgiving in every
  brew method. If you do not know where to start, start here. 250g $16, 1kg $52.
- Ironwood. Dark roast, smoky and heavy, built for milk and for people who grew up on
  dark coffee. 250g $16, 1kg $52.
- Nightjar Espresso. Our espresso blend. Medium-dark, caramel and toasted almond, holds
  up under milk but is sweet enough to drink straight. 250g $18, 1kg $58.
- Quiet Hours Decaf. Swiss Water process, medium roast, cocoa and dried fruit. 250g $17,
  1kg $55.

The single origins:
- Meridian Ethiopia. Yirgacheffe, natural process, light roast. Blueberry, jasmine, tea
  like body. Certified organic. 250g $21.
- Silver Fir Colombia. Huila, washed, medium roast. Red apple, caramel, clean finish.
  Certified organic. 250g $19.
- Kestrel Kenya. Nyeri, washed, light-medium roast. Blackcurrant, tomato, very bright.
  Certified organic. 250g $22.
- Redwood Brazil. Cerrado, pulped natural, medium-dark roast. Peanut, milk chocolate, low
  acidity. Certified organic. 250g $18.
- Lantern Guatemala. Huehuetenango, washed, medium roast. Toffee, red grape, cocoa. 250g $19.
- Cascade Sumatra. Mandheling, wet hulled, dark roast. Cedar, tobacco, earthy and syrupy.
  250g $19.
- Harbour Rwanda. Bourbon variety, washed, light-medium roast. Peach, black tea, floral.
  250g $20.

Q: Which coffee should I start with?
A: Hearth Blend. It works in every brew method, it is hard to ruin, and it is the coffee we
judge everything else against. If you already know you like bright, fruity coffee, start with
Meridian Ethiopia instead. If you like dark and heavy, start with Ironwood.

Q: Are your beans organic?
A: Four of our seven single origins are certified organic: Meridian Ethiopia, Silver Fir
Colombia, Kestrel Kenya and Redwood Brazil. Our blends are not certified because they contain
components from farms that do not carry certification, even though several of them farm
organically in practice. Certification costs money that a small farm often cannot justify.

Q: Are your beans fair trade?
A: We are not fair trade certified. All of our coffee is bought through direct trade, which
means we or our importing partner deal with the farm or the co-op directly, we know the farm
name, and we pay above the fair trade minimum in every single case. The price we paid per
pound of green coffee is listed on each product page. We think that is more useful than a
logo.

Q: What is the difference between a single origin and a blend?
A: A single origin comes from one farm, one co-op or one region, so it tastes distinctly of
that place and it changes as the harvest changes. A blend mixes coffees from several places
so it tastes consistent all year round. Single origins are for exploring. Blends are for
having the same good cup every morning.

Q: Do you sell green (unroasted) beans?
A: No. We have had a few requests and we may do a small green offering in future, but
everything on the site today is roasted.

Q: Do you offer decaf?
A: Yes, Quiet Hours. It is decaffeinated using the Swiss Water process, which uses only water
and carbon filters, no chemical solvents. It removes about 99.9 percent of the caffeine.

Q: How much caffeine is in your coffee?
A: A 250ml cup made at our standard 1:16 ratio contains roughly 95 to 140mg of caffeine
depending on the coffee. Light roasts have marginally more caffeine by weight than dark
roasts, though the difference is far smaller than the internet claims. Quiet Hours Decaf has
roughly 2 to 4mg per cup.

Q: Do you sell ground coffee or only whole bean?
A: We sell whole bean by default and we would gently push you to keep it that way, because
ground coffee goes stale in days rather than weeks. That said, we will grind it for you free
of charge if you ask. At checkout there is a grind dropdown: whole bean, espresso, moka pot,
pour over, drip, French press, or cold brew. Choose the one matching your brewer.

Q: Do you have a coffee of the month or rotating offering?
A: Yes, our Roaster's Pick rotates every 4 to 6 weeks and is usually a microlot too small to
put in the permanent range. It is announced in the newsletter and it sells out most times.

Q: Can I buy sample sizes?
A: We sell a Try Three pack: any three 100g bags for $22. It is the cheapest way to work out
what you like.

Q: How should I store my coffee?
A: Sealed, at room temperature, out of direct sunlight. Our bags have a one-way valve that
lets carbon dioxide out without letting oxygen in, which makes the bag itself the best
container you own. Do not decant into a glass jar on a sunny counter, however nice it looks.

Q: Should I keep coffee in the fridge or freezer?
A: Not the fridge, ever. It is humid and full of smells, and coffee absorbs both. The freezer
is acceptable only for unopened bags you are storing long term, sealed, and only if you thaw
the bag fully before opening it so condensation does not form on the beans. Once a bag is
open, keep it out of the freezer.

Q: My bag arrived puffed up like a balloon. Is it faulty?
A: No, it is the opposite. That is carbon dioxide escaping from very fresh coffee and pushing
the bag out through the valve. A puffy bag is a sign the coffee is fresh.

Q: Is your packaging recyclable?
A: Our bags are made from a recyclable LDPE laminate and are accepted in store drop off
recycling for soft plastics, which is not the same as kerbside. The valve is part of the bag
and does not need removing. Our boxes are unbleached cardboard and fully kerbside recyclable,
and we pack with paper, never plastic pillows. We are not going to pretend the bag is perfect.
It is the best barrier we have found that does not ruin the coffee.

SECTION 3: GRINDERS AND BREWING GEAR
================================================================================

Q: Which grinder should I buy for espresso?
A: The Ember Hand Grinder ($189) or the Ember Electric ($349). Espresso needs a grinder that
goes genuinely fine and, more importantly, repeats the same grind shot after shot. Both Embers
do this.

Q: Can I use the Daybreak Grinder for espresso?
A: No, and we would rather tell you now than take your money. The Daybreak ($89) does not go
fine enough for true espresso and no technique will rescue that. It is an excellent grinder
for pour over, drip, French press, moka pot and cold brew. If espresso is your plan, go
Ember.

Q: Which grinder should I buy for French press or pour over?
A: Any of the three will do these well. The Daybreak is the most affordable and completely
capable. The Embers are more consistent and faster, but the difference in the cup for
filter brewing is much smaller than it is for espresso.

Q: What is the difference between the Ember Hand Grinder and the Ember Electric?
A: The same 38mm conical burrs and the same grind quality. The hand grinder takes about
30 seconds of arm work for 18g and costs $189. The electric does it in 8 seconds, has 40
click stops, and costs $349. Buy the hand grinder if you make one or two cups a day. Buy
the electric if you make more, or if you find the ritual annoying rather than pleasant.

Q: Burr or blade grinder?
A: All three of our grinders are burr grinders. A blade grinder chops beans randomly into a
mix of dust and boulders, and there is no setting that fixes that. We do not sell blade
grinders and we would recommend a $89 burr grinder over a $200 blade one every time.

Q: Do you sell replacement burrs?
A: Yes, for the Ember Hand Grinder and the Ember Electric. They share the same 38mm burr set,
$45, listed under Accessories. We do not stock replacement burrs for the Daybreak. Burrs last
roughly 500 to 800kg of coffee, so for most home users that is many years.

Q: How do I clean my grinder?
A: Brush the burrs out weekly with the brush included in the box. Every few months, take the
burrs out and clean them properly. Do not put burrs in water and do not put any part of a
grinder in the dishwasher. Grinder cleaning tablets work fine and are the easy option if you
do not want to disassemble.

Q: Are your kettles suitable for induction hobs?
A: The Stone Gooseneck Kettle ($65) works on gas, electric and induction. The Copper Gooseneck
Kettle ($95) is not induction compatible because copper is not ferrous. The Stone Electric
Kettle ($119) does not need a hob at all.

Q: Do your kettles have temperature control?
A: The Stone Electric Kettle has variable temperature control from 40C to 100C (104F to 212F)
in 1 degree steps, and will hold a set temperature for up to 60 minutes. The two stovetop
kettles have no temperature control. If you buy a stovetop kettle, boil and wait 30 to 45
seconds with the lid off, which lands you near 93C.

Q: Why a gooseneck kettle?
A: The narrow spout gives you a slow, controllable stream, which matters enormously for pour
over and not at all for French press. If you only make French press or use a drip machine,
any kettle you already own is fine and you should spend the money on beans.

Q: What size are your kettles?
A: The Stone Gooseneck and Copper Gooseneck are 1 litre. The Stone Electric is 900ml usable.

Q: Do you sell scales?
A: Yes, the Hearthstone Scale, $55. It reads to 0.1g, has a built in timer, and switches off
after 5 minutes. Any scale that reads 0.1g and can take 2kg will work fine, though, and we
would rather you bought a cheap one than none.

Q: Do I really need a scale?
A: More than you need a nicer grinder. Coffee scoops vary by up to 30 percent depending on
the bean and how you dip. Weighing removes the single biggest source of inconsistency in
home brewing. It is the cheapest upgrade available.

Q: What brewers do you sell?
A: The V60 02 dripper in ceramic ($28), the Hearthstone French Press 800ml ($45), and paper
filters for the V60 in packs of 100 ($9). We do not sell espresso machines, drip machines,
moka pots or Aeropress, though our coffee works fine in all of them.

Q: What warranty comes with the gear?
A: Grinders and the Stone Electric Kettle carry a 2 year manufacturer warranty. Stovetop
kettles, brewers and scales carry a 1 year warranty. Warranty covers manufacturing defects.
It does not cover normal wear, accidental damage, or a grinder full of coffee that has turned
to concrete because it was never cleaned.

Q: Something broke after the warranty ended. Can you help?
A: Email us. We keep spares for most things and we will sell you the part at cost. We would
much rather fix a grinder than sell you a new one.

Q: Do you sell gear you do not stock?
A: No, but we are happy to tell you what we use at home if you email and ask. We have no
affiliate deals with anyone.

SECTION 4: SUBSCRIPTIONS
================================================================================

Q: How does the coffee subscription work?
A: You choose the coffee, the bag size and the frequency: every week, every 2 weeks, or every
4 weeks. We roast on your schedule and ship the same day. Your card is charged the morning of
your roast day, not when you sign up and not in advance.

Q: Do subscribers get a discount?
A: Yes. 10 percent off every bag and free standard shipping within the United States, on every
delivery, with no minimum order value.

Q: Does the subscription free shipping apply to Canada or international?
A: No. Free shipping on subscriptions applies to United States addresses only. Canadian and
international subscribers pay the standard flat rate on each delivery.

Q: Can I pause my subscription?
A: Yes, any time, from your account page. Pausing simply skips your next roast day. There is
no limit to how long you can pause and no penalty.

Q: Can I cancel my subscription?
A: Yes, any time, from your account page. There is no minimum number of deliveries, no notice
period and no cancellation fee. If you cancel after your coffee has been roasted for that
cycle, that final bag still ships and is charged, because it has already been made for you.

Q: Can I change which coffee I get?
A: Yes. Log in and switch it any time before your roast day. Changes made after the coffee is
roasted apply to the following delivery.

Q: Can I get a different coffee every time?
A: Yes, choose Roaster's Choice on the subscription page and we will send something different
each time, avoiding repeats and skipping anything you have told us you dislike. About a third
of our subscribers do this.

Q: Can I skip one delivery without pausing?
A: Yes, there is a Skip Next button on your account page.

Q: Can I change my subscription frequency?
A: Yes, from your account page, any time. The most common mistake is signing up for weekly
when you drink a bag a fortnight, then drowning in coffee. Start at 4 weeks, and speed it up
if you run out.

Q: I have too much coffee. What now?
A: Pause or skip, and consider moving to a longer frequency or a bigger bag less often. Coffee
you do not drink is money on the counter.

Q: Can I gift a subscription?
A: Yes, 3, 6 or 12 month prepaid gift subscriptions. They do not auto renew, so nobody gets an
unexpected charge, and we can send a note with the first bag if you give us the wording at
checkout.

Q: When exactly will my subscription ship?
A: On the next roast day, which is Tuesday or Friday. You will get a shipping email with
tracking the moment the label prints.

SECTION 6: BREWING QUESTIONS WE GET A LOT
================================================================================

Q: What grind setting should I use?
A: It depends on your grinder, so we cannot give you a number. Use the shape instead:
espresso is powdered sugar, moka pot is table salt, pour over is coarse sand, drip is beach
sand, French press is breadcrumbs, cold brew is cracked pepper. Full detail is in our
brewing guide.

Q: What water temperature?
A: 90C to 96C (195F to 205F). Our house target is 93C (200F). Light roasts like the top of
that range, dark roasts prefer the bottom.

Q: What ratio of coffee to water?
A: 1:16 for pour over and drip, 1:15 for French press, 1:2 for espresso, 1:7 for moka pot,
1:8 for cold brew concentrate. Weigh both.

Q: My coffee tastes sour. What is wrong?
A: It is under-extracted, which means the water did not pull enough out of the grounds. Grind
finer, or brew hotter, or brew longer. Change one at a time.

Q: My coffee tastes bitter. What is wrong?
A: It is over-extracted, the opposite problem. Grind coarser, or brew cooler, or brew shorter.

Q: My coffee tastes like nothing at all.
A: Check the roast date and check your ratio. Flat and aromaless usually means stale beans.
Weak but still aromatic usually means you did not use enough coffee.

Q: Can I use tap water?
A: If it tastes good to drink, yes. Filtered is better. Distilled or zero TDS water is worse
than tap, because coffee needs some mineral content to extract properly.

Q: Is your coffee good in an Aeropress / moka pot / Nespresso style machine?
A: Aeropress and moka pot, absolutely. Our coffee is not available in capsules and we have no
plans to change that.

Q: Which coffee for milk drinks?
A: Nightjar Espresso first, Ironwood second, Redwood Brazil third. Light and fruity coffees
tend to disappear under milk.

Q: Which coffee for cold brew?
A: Redwood Brazil or Ironwood. Cold brew rewards chocolatey, low acidity coffees. Do not
waste your Kestrel Kenya on it.
